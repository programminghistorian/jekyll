The English-language edition of *[Programming Historian](/en)* is seeking new editors to join its editorial team!

This is an opportunity to become part of a global and interdisciplinary team of volunteers who are passionate about developing and sharing practical learning materials on digital methods and workflows that are open, accessible, and sustainable.

## About *Programming Historian*

*Programming Historian* is an open-access, multilingual, and interdisciplinary publishing project that offers peer-reviewed lessons on a wide range of digital tools, methods, and workflows for students, researchers, and practitioners in the humanities and social sciences.

The English edition launched in 2012, and the project has expanded in recent years to include [Spanish](/es/), [French](/fr/), and [Portuguese](/pt) editions. Each edition publishes both original lessons and translations of lessons from the other language editions. 

*Programming Historian* is a volunteer-led initiative, supported by the Board of ProgHist Ltd, a registered charity and not-for-profit company, and by community contributors.

## Key Responsibilities

As a member of *Programming Historian in English* editorial team, your key responsibilities include:

- Participating in the review and selection of lesson proposals
- Editing a minimum of one lesson or translation per year

The workload varies in intensity throughout the year. The busiest periods tend to be in February/March, when lesson proposals are reviewed; in April/May, when initial editing takes place; and in September/October when finalising lessons for publication. We ask editors to remain communicative and responsive throughout the year, and to participate in occasional team meetings. Editors may also have opportunities to collaborate with other members of *Programming Historian* teams on research projects or presentations, depending on interest and availability. 

This is an unpaid volunteer position. You will be working remotely within an international team.

## Skills and Competencies

**Essential**

- Experience in learning, developing, and applying digital methods in the humanities and social sciences, broadly construed
- Familiarity with open-source tools, methods, and data
- Self-motivated and reliable with the ability to work both independently and collaboratively
- Familiarity with *Programming Historian* and an affinity with its goals and values

**Desirable**

- Spanish, Portuguese, and/or French language skills
- Experience in teaching or developing learning materials involving digital methods
- Experience in digital methods that engage with multimodal analysis, sound/audio, moving images, computer vision, or LLMs (especially open-source and creative or critical approaches to LLMs)
- Experience with digital methods in libraries, archives, and cultural collections
- Editorial experience

We encourage you to apply even if you do not meet all criteria listed above. We value diversity of skills, perspectives, and lived experiences, and are continually seeking to expand the range of topics, practices, and communities represented in our lessons. We are particularly interested in hearing about the skills and perspectives you would bring to the editorial team.

We are committed to diversity and equal access in digital education, and we particularly welcome applications from women, members of marginalised groups, LGBTQ+ people, and people from the Global South. With this call, we strongly encourage the participation of Anglophone and multilingual communities beyond North America.

Laura Alice Chapot (Managing Editor), Caio Mello (Assistant Managing Editor), and Anisa Hawes (Publishing Manager) will offer training and support to new editors on *Programming Historian*’s editorial processes, approaches, and policies.

## How to Apply

If you are interested in applying, please fill out the [application form](https://tinyurl.com/editor-call-2026) and provide a CV of no more than two pages by **23 December 2026** to tell us about your relevant interests, skills, and experience.

If you’d like to ask any questions about this role, please write to the Managing Editor, [Laura Alice Chapot](mailto:english@programminghistorian.org), or our Publishing Manager, [Anisa Hawes](mailto:admin@programminghistorian.org).

We look forward to reading your applications!
